Four Skills, One Habit:
Turn It Into ONE Number

Roughly one question in four on the CJMC (outside geometry and counting) is a rate, ratio, percent or mean word problem — and it’s the biggest group on the Gauss too. These are points you win by staying calm and organised. Let’s play with each idea first, then beat the real contest questions.

1 · RatesFind the amount for ONE, then scale. 2 · RatioCount the parts, then share the whole. 3 · PercentA percent is a multiplier. 4 · MeanWork with the total, not the average.

⚡
Find the amount for ONE first.km per hour, pages per day, dollars per item… then scale up or down.
🖨️ Lab 1 · The printer

A printer prints 12 pages in 4 minutes. Can you work out how long any job takes?

🚶‍♀️ Lab 2 · Elizabeth walks home Easy
CJMC (Canada Jay) 2022 — Q3. Elizabeth walks at a constant speed. She walks [[4/5]] of the way home in 40 minutes. How long will it take for her to walk the rest of the way?
(A) 6 minutes(B) 8 minutes(C) 10 minutes(D) 32 minutes(E) 50 minutes
Speed = distance ÷ time. If the speed is constant, equal pieces of the way take equal time.
🚙 Lab 3 · Meet-up or catch-up?

Two cars start gap km apart. Drag the sliders, press Go, and watch the gap shrink. Flip the switch to make them drive the same way.

Contest time

Gauss 2017, Grade 8 — Q18 Medium
Brodie and Ryan are driving directly towards each other. Brodie is driving at a constant speed of 50 km/h. Ryan is driving at a constant speed of 40 km/h. If they are 120 km apart, how long will it take before they meet?
(A) 1 h 12 min(B) 1 h 25 min(C) 1 h 15 min(D) 1 h 33 min(E) 1 h 20 min
Meet-up: speed₁ + speed₂.   Catch-up: speed₁ − speed₂.   Speeds (rates) can be added or subtracted — as long as they are the same kind of quantity.
🍰
Level 2

Ratio: parts of a whole

🧩
Count the parts, then share the whole.a : b means “a parts for every b parts”. Total parts = a + b.
🍫 Lab 4 · Share the cake Easy
Gauss 2018, Grade 8 — Q8. Rich and Ben ate an entire chocolate cake. The ratio of the amount eaten by Rich to the amount eaten by Ben is 3 : 1. What percentage of the cake did Ben eat?
(A) 66%(B) 50%(C) 75%(D) 25%(E) 10%
3 : 1 is part-to-part (Rich to Ben). A fraction like [[1/4]] is part-to-whole. Always ask: which one is the question asking about?
🥗 Lab 5 · Change only one side Easy
Gauss 2023, Grade 8 — Q8. In a salad dressing, the ratio of oil to vinegar is 3 : 1. To make the dressing more acidic, the amount of vinegar is doubled. What is the new ratio of oil to vinegar?
(A) 3 : 2(B) 6 : 1(C) 1 : 3(D) 2 : 3(E) 4 : 3
Equivalent ratios: multiply or divide BOTH sides by the same number (3 : 1 = 6 : 2 = 9 : 3). Changing one amount? Change only that side, then re-read the ratio and simplify.

Level 3

Percent: a multiplier

✖️
A percent is a multiplier.30% of N → × 0.30 · reduced by 30% → × 0.70 · increased by 30% → × 1.30
⚡ Lab 6 · Multiplier lightning round

Turn each phrase into its multiplier. Get 6 right to finish the round.

🔗 Lab 7 · The price-chain builder

Start with a $100 price tag, then tap changes to chain them together. Successive changes MULTIPLY — they don’t add.

Contest time

Gauss 2021, Grade 7 — Q17 Medium
The original price of a shirt is reduced by 50% to obtain a second price. The store advertises an additional sale, and so this second price is reduced by 40% to obtain a third price. What is the discount of the third price off the original price?
(A) 80%(B) 10%(C) 70%(D) 65%(E) 45%
The original price isn’t given — so pick an easy one, like $100. (Be careful: the real price isn’t $100, but the percent answer is the same for any price!)

Level 4

Mean: total ÷ count

📦
Work with the total, not the average.Mean = sum ÷ how many. Flip it: sum = mean × count.
🧱 Lab 10 · Level it out

Each bar is a number. Tap a bar to pick it up, then tap another bar to give it one cube. Watch the orange mean line.

➖ Lab 11 · Take a number away Easy
Gauss 2016, Grade 7 — Q18. The mean (average) of a set of six numbers is 10. When the number 25 is removed from the set, the mean of the remaining numbers is
(A) 6(B) 7(C) 8(D) 9(E) 10
Adding or removing a value changes the total and the count — recompute from those.
📊 Lab 12 · Decode the cumulative mean Medium–Hard
CJMC (Canada Jay) 2022 — Q8. Zaria practiced ring toss by taking 15 tosses a day for 5 days. The graph is her cumulative mean (average) number of tosses scored each day. For example, the bar labelled 3 shows she had a mean of 6 tosses scored each day over the first 3 days. Which day did Zaria score the fewest number of her tosses?
(A) day 1(B) day 2(C) day 3(D) day 4(E) day 5
🪢 Lab 13 · Tug-of-war: combining two groups

Two classes took the same quiz. Never just average the averages! The bigger group pulls harder. Slide things around and see.

🎲 Lab 14 · Median & mode playground

Median = the middle value once the numbers are in order. Mode = the value that appears most often. Type some numbers and see.

The point to land

Every one of these problems becomes easy once you turn it into ONE number you can scale.

  • Rate → the amount for ONE
  • Ratio → the number of parts
  • Percent → the multiplier
  • Mean → the total
