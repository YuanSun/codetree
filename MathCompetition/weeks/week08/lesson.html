🚗 50 km/h ➜  ······ 120 km ······  ⬅ 40 km/h 🚙

Brodie and Ryan drive straight towards each other, 120 km apart. How long until they meet?

World 2

Ratio

Parts of a whole. a : b means “a parts for every b parts”.

🧩
Count the parts, then share the whole.Total parts = a + b.
🔍 Explore · The ratio scaler
🦊
Make more copies of a recipe. The numbers get bigger… but does the mix change? Slide to find out!
🍫 Lab 4 · Share the cake
📜 Gauss 2018 · Grade 8 · Q8Easy

Rich and Ben ate a whole cake. Rich : Ben = 3 : 1. What percent of the cake did Ben eat?

Show the exact contest wording
Rich and Ben ate an entire chocolate cake. The ratio of the amount eaten by Rich to the amount eaten by Ben is 3 : 1. What percentage of the cake did Ben eat?
(A) 66%(B) 50%(C) 75%(D) 25%(E) 10%
3 : 1 compares part to part. A fraction like [[1/4]] compares part to whole. Always ask: who is the question about?
🥗 Lab 5 · Change only one side
📜 Gauss 2023 · Grade 8 · Q8Easy

Oil : vinegar is 3 : 1. Make it more sour by doubling the vinegar. New ratio?

Show the exact contest wording
In a salad dressing, the ratio of oil to vinegar is 3 : 1. To make the dressing more acidic, the amount of vinegar is doubled. What is the new ratio of oil to vinegar?
(A) 3 : 2(B) 6 : 1(C) 1 : 3(D) 2 : 3(E) 4 : 3
Equivalent ratios: multiply or divide BOTH sides by the same number. Changing one amount? Change only that side, then re-read the ratio.

World 3

Percent

“Per cent” means out of 100. And that makes it a multiplier.

✖️
A percent is a multiplier.30% of N → × 0.30 · reduced by 30% → × 0.70 · increased by 30% → × 1.30
💯 Explore · The 100-square
🦊
100 little squares = the whole price. Pick “of”, “reduced” or “increased”, drag the slider, and watch the squares — and the multiplier.
⚡ Lab 6 · Multiplier lightning round

Turn each phrase into its multiplier. Get 6 right to finish.

🔗 Lab 7 · The price-chain builder

Start with a $100 tag. Tap changes to chain them. Successive changes MULTIPLY — they don’t add.

📜 Gauss 2021 · Grade 7 · Q17Medium
🏷️ 100% ➜ −50% ➜ −40% ➜ ?

A shirt’s price drops 50%, then drops another 40%. What’s the total discount off the original price?

Show the exact contest wording
The original price of a shirt is reduced by 50% to obtain a second price. The store advertises an additional sale, and so this second price is reduced by 40% to obtain a third price. What is the discount of the third price off the original price?
(A) 80%(B) 10%(C) 70%(D) 65%(E) 45%
No starting price given? Pick an easy one — $100. The percent answer is the same for any price.

World 4

Mean

The “fair share” number. Mean = total ÷ count.

📦
Work with the total, not the average.Flip it around: total = mean × count.
🎪 Explore · The balance beam
🦊
The mean is the exact spot where a see-saw balances! Drag the triangle until the beam is level. Then drag the weights and see what happens.
🧱 Lab 10 · Level it out

Tap a bar to pick it up, then tap another to give it one cube. Watch the orange mean line.

📜 Gauss 2016 · Grade 7 · Q18Easy

Six numbers have a mean of 10. Take away the number 25. What’s the new mean?

Show the exact contest wording
The mean (average) of a set of six numbers is 10. When the number 25 is removed from the set, the mean of the remaining numbers is
(A) 6(B) 7(C) 8(D) 9(E) 10
Adding or removing a value changes the total and the count — recompute from those.
📜 CJMC 2022 · Q8Medium–Hard

Zaria’s cumulative mean after each of 5 days is in the graph (after day 3 her mean was 6). Which day did she score the fewest tosses?

Show the exact contest wording
Zaria practiced ring toss by taking 15 tosses a day for 5 days. The graph is her cumulative mean (average) number of tosses scored each day. For example, the bar labelled 3 shows she had a mean of 6 tosses scored each day over the first 3 days. Which day did Zaria score the fewest number of her tosses?
(A) day 1(B) day 2(C) day 3(D) day 4(E) day 5
🪢 Lab 13 · Tug-of-war: combining two groups

Two classes took the same quiz. Never just average the averages! The bigger group pulls harder.

🎲 Lab 14 · Median & mode playground

Median = the middle value once the numbers are in order. Mode = the value that appears most often.

The point to land

Every one of these problems becomes easy once you turn it into ONE number you can scale.

  • Rate → the amount for ONE
  • Ratio → the number of parts
  • Percent → the multiplier
  • Mean → the total
