# Ethical and Professional Standards
@topic ethics

## Ethics and trust in the investment profession
@id ethics-trust
@r 93
@aka ethics; professional ethics; trust; code of conduct; why ethics matter; ethical decision making framework; challenges to ethical behaviour; overconfidence; situational influences
@see code-and-standards; ethical-decision-framework

Investment professionals are trusted with other people's money and information that clients can't easily check, so markets only work if the profession is trusted. A **profession** has specialized knowledge, admission standards, a code of conduct, monitoring and enforcement, and serves society beyond its members' self-interest.

Rules (laws, regulations) set minimum standards; ethics often asks for more. Legal is not always ethical, and ethical is not always written into law. Common obstacles to ethical behaviour: **overconfidence** in one's own morals, **situational influences** (bonuses, pressure from bosses, peers, the short term) and focusing on immediate rather than long-term consequences.

## Ethical decision-making framework
@id ethical-decision-framework
@r 93
@aka identify consider act reflect; ethical framework; how to make an ethical decision; ethical dilemma
@see ethics-trust

A structured process for difficult situations:

1. **Identify**: the facts, the stakeholders and duties owed, the ethical principles, and any conflicts of interest.
2. **Consider**: situational influences, extra guidance (colleagues, compliance, legal), and alternative actions.
3. **Decide and act.**
4. **Reflect**: was the outcome as expected? What would you do differently?

## CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards
@id code-and-standards
@r 94
@aka Code of Ethics; Standards of Professional Conduct; six components of the code; seven standards; professional conduct program; sanctions
@see standard-i-a; standard-vii-a

The **Code of Ethics** asks members and candidates to: act with integrity, competence, diligence, respect and ethically; put the integrity of the profession and clients' interests above their own; use reasonable care and independent judgment; act and encourage others to act professionally; promote the integrity of capital markets; and maintain and improve their competence.

The **Standards of Professional Conduct** are grouped in seven sections: I Professionalism, II Integrity of Capital Markets, III Duties to Clients, IV Duties to Employers, V Investment Analysis, Recommendations, and Actions, VI Conflicts of Interest, VII Responsibilities as a CFA Institute Member or Candidate.

The **Professional Conduct Program** investigates complaints (self-disclosure on the annual statement, written complaints, media reports, exam proctors). Sanctions range from a private reprimand to censure, suspension or revocation of membership and the charter.

> **At work** If local law and the Standards differ, follow the **stricter** one.

## Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law
@id standard-i-a
@r 95
@aka knowledge of the law; stricter of law or code; dissociate from violations; whistleblowing; participate or assist in violations
@see code-and-standards; standard-i-d

Understand and comply with all applicable laws, rules and regulations, including the Code and Standards. Where they conflict, follow the **stricter**. Do not knowingly participate in or assist violations, and **dissociate** from any violation (e.g. refuse to work on the matter, and if necessary leave the firm). Report concerns to a supervisor or compliance first. You are not required to report violations to regulators, though it may be appropriate.

## Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity
@id standard-i-b
@r 95
@aka independence; objectivity; gifts; entertainment; issuer paid research; travel paid by company; pressure from investment banking; modest gifts
@see standard-vi-a

Use reasonable care to keep independence and objectivity, and don't offer, solicit or accept any gift, benefit or consideration that could compromise your own or someone else's independence.

- Modest gifts and entertainment are acceptable. Gifts from **clients** (for past performance) are generally fine if disclosed to the employer; gifts from **others** seeking to influence you are more troubling.
- Pay for your own travel to company visits where possible; avoid lavish hospitality.
- Issuer-paid research: payment should be a flat fee, not tied to the conclusion, and must be disclosed.
- Investment banking pressure on research analysts must be resisted (firewalls).

## Standard I(C) Misrepresentation
@id standard-i-c
@r 95
@aka misrepresentation; plagiarism; guaranteed returns; false statements; copying research; credit to sources
@see standard-iii-d; standard-vii-b

Do not knowingly make misrepresentations about investment analysis, recommendations, actions or professional activities. This includes **plagiarism**: copying or using others' work without credit. You may use widely recognized public sources (e.g. government statistics) without attribution, but name the analysts whose work you quote. Do not guarantee investment returns on inherently risky products (a guaranteed product that really is guaranteed may be described accurately).

## Standard I(D) Misconduct
@id standard-i-d
@r 95
@aka misconduct; dishonesty; fraud; deceit; personal behavior; reflect adversely on professional reputation
@see standard-i-a

Do not engage in conduct involving dishonesty, fraud or deceit, or commit any act that reflects adversely on your professional reputation, integrity or competence. Purely personal behaviour that has nothing to do with professional integrity (e.g. a civil disobedience protest) is generally not a violation.

## Standard I(E) Competence
@id standard-i-e
@r 95
@aka competence; continuing education; skills; act with competence; professional development
@see code-and-standards

Act with and strive to maintain and improve the competence needed for your professional activities. Understand the products you recommend; don't take on work you are not qualified for without getting help.

## Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information
@id standard-ii-a
@r 96
@aka insider trading; MNPI; material nonpublic information; mosaic theory; information barrier; firewall; restricted list; watch list; selective disclosure
@see standard-ii-b; mosaic-theory

If you have **material nonpublic information** that could affect the value of an investment, do not act on it or cause others to act on it.

- **Material**: a reasonable investor would want to know it, or it would likely move the price. Reliability of the source matters.
- **Nonpublic**: not yet disseminated to the market at large.

Firms should use **information barriers (firewalls)**, restricted and watch lists, and pre-clearance of personal trades. If you receive MNPI, encourage the issuer to make it public, and don't trade.

## Mosaic theory
@id mosaic-theory
@r 96
@aka mosaic theory; combining public information; nonmaterial nonpublic information; analyst research; conclusions from pieces
@see standard-ii-a

An analyst may reach a conclusion that would be material if disclosed by the company, by combining **public** information with **nonmaterial nonpublic** information. Acting on that conclusion is not a violation of II(A). Keep records of the research to show how the conclusion was reached.

## Standard II(B) Market Manipulation
@id standard-ii-b
@r 96
@aka market manipulation; pump and dump; spreading false rumors; wash trading; cornering the market; artificial prices; volume manipulation
@see standard-ii-a

Do not engage in practices that distort prices or artificially inflate trading volume with intent to mislead. Two types: **information-based** (spreading false rumours, "pump and dump") and **transaction-based** (trades that create a misleading impression of price or volume, cornering a market). Legitimate trading strategies, such as minimizing tax or using an exchange's market-making programme within its rules, are fine; **intent to mislead** is the key.

## Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care
@id standard-iii-a
@r 97
@aka fiduciary duty; loyalty to clients; prudence; care; proxy voting; soft dollars; client brokerage; who is the client; best interest
@see standard-iii-b; standard-iii-c

Act for the benefit of clients and place their interests before your employer's and your own. Act with the care, skill and diligence a prudent person would use.

- Identify **who the client is** (e.g. for a pension plan, the beneficiaries).
- **Client brokerage (soft dollars)** belongs to the client; use it only for research that benefits that client.
- **Proxy voting** is part of managing client assets; vote in the client's interest (a cost-benefit judgment is allowed for minor issues).
- Follow the investment mandate; manage the whole portfolio in the context of the client's objectives.

## Standard III(B) Fair Dealing
@id standard-iii-b
@r 97
@aka fair dealing; treat all clients fairly; allocation of trades; IPO allocation; pro rata allocation; disseminating recommendations; hot issues
@see standard-iii-a; standard-vi-b

Deal fairly and objectively with all clients when giving analysis, making recommendations, taking investment action or in other professional activities. **Fair** does not mean identical: different service levels are allowed if disclosed and available to all who pay for them, but no client may be disadvantaged. Disseminate changes in recommendations to all clients at the same time; allocate block trades and oversubscribed IPOs **pro rata**, and don't take shares in hot issues for yourself or family at clients' expense.

## Standard III(C) Suitability
@id standard-iii-c
@r 97
@aka suitability; know your client; investment policy statement; unsolicited trade; risk tolerance; client objectives and constraints; mandate
@see standard-iii-a; ips

In an advisory relationship: make reasonable inquiry into the client's experience, risk and return objectives and constraints before acting, and **update** at least annually; judge suitability in the context of the whole portfolio. If a client asks for an unsuitable trade (**unsolicited**), discuss it; if it materially changes the portfolio's risk, you may need to update the IPS or get the client's acknowledgment. When managing to a mandate (e.g. a fund), only make investments consistent with the stated mandate.

## Standard III(D) Performance Presentation
@id standard-iii-d
@r 97
@aka performance presentation; cherry picking; simulated results; composite; GIPS; misleading performance; past performance disclosure
@see gips; standard-i-c

Make reasonable efforts to ensure performance information is **fair, accurate and complete**. Don't show only your best accounts or periods; label simulated or back-tested results; disclose whether returns are gross or net of fees; and if presenting a composite, include all similar accounts (including terminated ones). Following [[gips]] is the recommended way to comply.

## Standard III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality
@id standard-iii-e
@r 97
@aka confidentiality; client information; privacy; data protection; former clients
@see standard-iii-a

Keep information about current, former and prospective clients confidential, unless it concerns illegal activity by the client, disclosure is required by law, or the client permits it. Cooperating with a CFA Institute Professional Conduct investigation is allowed.

## Standard IV(A) Loyalty (to employer)
@id standard-iv-a
@r 98
@aka loyalty to employer; leaving a firm; soliciting clients; taking client lists; whistleblowing; independent practice; competing with employer
@see standard-iv-b; standard-iv-c

Act for the benefit of your employer and do not deprive it of your skills, divulge its confidential information or otherwise cause it harm. When planning to leave you may prepare (arrange office space, register a new company) but **must not** solicit clients, take records or client lists, or compete before you leave. After leaving, you may contact former clients using public information and memory, unless a non-compete agreement says otherwise. Whistleblowing to protect clients or market integrity is not a breach.

## Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements
@id standard-iv-b
@r 98
@aka additional compensation; outside compensation; bonus from client; side work; written consent from employer
@see standard-iv-a

Do not accept gifts, benefits or compensation that compete with or might create a conflict with your employer's interest unless you get **written consent** from everyone involved (your employer). For example, a client's offer of a bonus for beating a benchmark requires disclosure and consent.

## Standard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors
@id standard-iv-c
@r 98
@aka supervisor responsibilities; compliance system; reasonable efforts to prevent violations; delegation; compliance procedures
@see standard-iv-a

Make reasonable efforts to ensure anyone under your supervision complies with laws, rules and the Standards. Supervisors should make sure an adequate **compliance system** exists, enforce it, and respond to violations promptly (investigate, restrict activities). If the firm's compliance system is inadequate, decline supervisory responsibility in writing until it is fixed.

## Standard V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis
@id standard-v-a
@r 99
@aka diligence; reasonable basis; due diligence; research quality; relying on third party research; quantitative models; group research
@see standard-v-b; standard-v-c

Exercise diligence, independence and thoroughness in analysis, and have a **reasonable and adequate basis**, supported by research and investigation, for any recommendation or action. You may rely on others' research if you've assessed its quality. If you disagree with a group's consensus recommendation, you may still allow your name on it if the process was sound; document your dissent. Understand the models and external managers you use.

## Standard V(B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients
@id standard-v-b
@r 99
@aka communication with clients; distinguish fact from opinion; disclose investment process; risks and limitations; changes in process
@see standard-v-a; standard-v-c

Disclose to clients the basic format and general principles of the investment process and promptly disclose material changes; use judgment to identify the important factors; distinguish **fact from opinion**; and explain **significant risks and limitations** of the analysis and strategy.

## Standard V(C) Record Retention
@id standard-v-c
@r 99
@aka record retention; keeping records; documentation; seven years; research files
@see standard-v-a

Develop and maintain appropriate records supporting investment analysis, recommendations, actions and client communications. Records belong to the firm. If no regulatory requirement exists, CFA Institute recommends keeping them at least **seven years**.

## Standard VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts
@id standard-vi-a
@r 100
@aka conflicts of interest; disclose conflicts; stock ownership conflict; board membership; compensation conflicts; referral arrangements disclosure
@see standard-vi-b; standard-vi-c; standard-i-b

Make full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably impair your independence and objectivity or interfere with duties to clients, prospects and your employer. Disclosures must be **prominent**, in plain language, and effectively communicated. Examples: owning the stock you recommend, serving on a company's board, investment-banking relationships, compensation tied to sales.

## Standard VI(B) Priority of Transactions
@id standard-vi-b
@r 100
@aka front running; personal trading; priority of transactions; client trades first; blackout period; preclearance; beneficial ownership
@see standard-vi-a; standard-iii-b

Client and employer transactions come before transactions in which you are a beneficial owner. Don't **front-run** client orders. Family accounts that are clients should be treated like any other client (not disadvantaged). Firms use blackout periods, pre-clearance and reporting of personal holdings to enforce this.

## Standard VI(C) Referral Fees
@id standard-vi-c
@r 100
@aka referral fees; referral arrangements; finder's fees; disclose referral compensation
@see standard-vi-a

Disclose to your employer, clients and prospects any compensation, consideration or benefit received from or paid to others for the recommendation of products or services, before the client enters the agreement, including its nature and value.

## Standard VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs
@id standard-vii-a
@r 101
@aka exam conduct; cheating; revealing exam questions; confidentiality of the CFA exam; misuse of CFA designation
@see standard-vii-b

Do not engage in conduct that compromises the reputation or integrity of CFA Institute, the CFA designation or the integrity, validity or security of its programmes. Examples: cheating, discussing specific exam questions or topics (beyond broad topic areas) after the exam, misrepresenting information on the annual Professional Conduct Statement. Expressing opinions about CFA Institute is allowed.

## Standard VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program
@id standard-vii-b
@r 101
@aka using CFA designation; CFA charterholder; candidate in the CFA program; CFA level; misuse of CFA marks; Level III candidate
@see standard-vii-a

Don't misrepresent or exaggerate the meaning or implications of CFA Institute membership, the charter or candidacy.

- Use "CFA" only as an adjective ("CFA charterholder", "CFA designation"), never as a noun or with periods ("C.F.A.") or in a firm's name.
- Don't claim superior performance because you hold the charter.
- A candidate may state they are a "Level II candidate in the CFA Program", but there is no "Level I CFA" designation and no "CFA, expected 2028".
- Membership lapses if dues and the annual conduct statement are not filed.

## Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS)
@id gips
@r 97, 102
@aka GIPS; performance standards; composites; firm wide compliance; verification; GIPS compliance claim
@see standard-iii-d; mwr-twr

Voluntary, global standards for calculating and presenting investment performance so prospective clients can compare managers fairly. Key ideas:

- Compliance is claimed by a **firm**, firm-wide; partial compliance is not allowed.
- All actual fee-paying discretionary portfolios must be included in at least one **composite** (portfolios with similar strategies).
- Prescribed return calculations (time-weighted returns for most composites) and required disclosures.
- **Verification** by an independent third party is recommended, not required.
