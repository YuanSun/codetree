## Commodity sectors and life cycle
@id commodity-sectors
@r 30
@aka commodity sectors; energy commodities; industrial metals; precious metals; grains; livestock; softs; commodity life cycle; production and consumption cycle; commodity supply chain
@see commodities; commodity-futures-pricing

Sectors: **energy** (crude oil, natural gas, refined products), **industrial metals**, **precious metals**, **grains** (corn, wheat, soybeans), **livestock**, **softs** (coffee, cocoa, sugar, cotton). They differ in storability, seasonality, supply flexibility and sensitivity to the economy and weather.

Life cycles vary: grains follow planting and harvest seasons; livestock follows breeding and feeding cycles; energy and metals have long investment lead times and continuous production. Understanding the cycle explains inventory levels and the shape of the futures curve.

## Valuing commodities vs. stocks and bonds
@id commodity-valuation
@r 30
@aka commodity valuation; no cash flows commodities; commodity price drivers; supply and demand commodities; commodity market participants; hedgers speculators arbitrageurs
@see commodity-sectors; commodity-futures-pricing

Commodities generate **no cash flows**, so they can't be valued by DCF. Prices reflect current and expected supply and demand, storage costs, inventories and the convenience of holding them. Futures markets participants: **hedgers** (producers and users), **speculators** (provide liquidity, take price risk), **arbitrageurs** (exploit spot–futures mispricing with physical inventory), exchanges, analysts and regulators.

## Contango, backwardation and futures return theories
@id commodity-futures-pricing
@r 30
@aka contango; backwardation; insurance theory; normal backwardation; hedging pressure hypothesis; theory of storage; convenience yield; futures price vs spot price commodities
@see cost-of-carry; commodity-returns

- **Backwardation**: futures below spot (common when inventories are tight; high convenience yield).
- **Contango**: futures above spot (ample inventories; storage and financing costs dominate).

Theories of futures returns:

- **Insurance theory (Keynes)**: producers hedge by selling futures, paying speculators a premium → **normal backwardation**; futures prices rise toward spot over time.
- **Hedging pressure hypothesis**: if consumers' hedging dominates, the curve can be in contango.
- **Theory of storage**: futures price = spot + storage costs − convenience yield; low inventories → high convenience yield → backwardation.

## Total return on commodity futures
@id commodity-returns
@r 30
@aka commodity futures total return; roll return; roll yield; spot price return; collateral return; rebalance return; fully collateralized futures
@see commodity-futures-pricing; commodities

Total return on a **fully collateralized** futures position = **price return** (change in futures price) + **roll return** + **collateral return** (interest on cash backing the position), plus a **rebalance return** for indexes. Roll return is positive in **backwardation** (roll into cheaper contracts) and negative in **contango**. Over long periods, roll return has been a major source of differences between commodity index returns and spot price changes.

## Commodity swaps and indexes
@id commodity-swaps-indexes
@r 30
@aka commodity swap; total return swap commodity; excess return swap; basis swap; variance swap commodities; commodity index construction; S&P GSCI; Bloomberg commodity index; index weighting commodities
@see commodity-returns; index-weighting

**Commodity swaps** give or modify exposure without trading futures: **total return swaps** (receive the index total return, pay a rate plus fee), **excess return swaps** (pay only a fixed fee; receive index return above a level), **basis swaps** (exchange returns on two related commodities), **variance/volatility swaps**.

**Index construction** affects returns: weighting method (production-weighted vs liquidity or fixed weights; energy-heavy vs diversified), rolling methodology (which contracts and when), and rebalancing frequency.

## Real estate investment features and value drivers
@id real-estate-l2
@r 31
@aka real estate features; property types; office; retail; industrial; multifamily; hospitality; lease structures; real estate economic drivers; role in portfolio; real estate value drivers
@see real-estate; real-estate-valuation-l2

Value drivers by property type: **office** (job growth in office-using sectors, lease length), **retail** (consumer spending, location, percentage rents), **industrial/logistics** (trade and e-commerce), **multifamily** (population, household formation, affordability), **hospitality** (travel demand; operating business with daily "leases"). Real estate offers income, potential inflation protection, and diversification, but is illiquid, lumpy and management-intensive.

## Real estate valuation approaches and due diligence
@id real-estate-valuation-l2
@r 31
@aka real estate appraisal; income approach; direct capitalization; cap rate; DCF real estate; discount rate vs cap rate; cost approach; sales comparison approach; due diligence real estate; appraisal based indexes
@see real-estate-valuation; reit-valuation

- **Income approach**: **direct capitalization** \(V = NOI_1 / \text{cap rate}\), where cap rate ≈ discount rate − growth; or a **DCF** of NOI with a terminal value.
- **Cost approach**: land value + replacement cost of building − depreciation (physical, functional, external obsolescence).
- **Sales comparison approach**: recent sales of comparable properties, adjusted for differences.

**Due diligence**: leases and rent roll, operating expenses, environmental and engineering inspections, title and legal, zoning, and market review.

**Indexes**: appraisal-based indexes lag and smooth values (understate volatility and correlation); transaction-based indexes have sample-selection bias; REIT indexes are timely but reflect leverage and equity-market sentiment.

## Publicly traded real estate securities
@id public-real-estate-securities
@r 32
@aka REITs types; equity REITs; mortgage REITs; real estate operating companies; REOCs; residential MBS; commercial MBS; public vs private real estate
@see reit; reit-valuation

Types: **REITs** (pass-through tax treatment for distributing most income; equity REITs own properties, mortgage REITs hold loans), **real estate operating companies** (REOCs: no distribution requirement, taxed as corporations, more flexibility), and **mortgage-backed securities**.

**Advantages** of public securities over private ownership: liquidity, diversification, professional management, transparency, lower minimum investment, limited liability. **Disadvantages**: share prices driven by equity markets, less control, structural costs, and REIT payout requirements limit retained earnings for growth.

## REIT valuation: NAVPS, FFO and AFFO multiples, DCF
@id reit-valuation
@r 32
@aka REIT valuation; net asset value per share; NAVPS; FFO; AFFO; price to FFO; price to AFFO; funds from operations; adjusted funds from operations; REIT DDM
@see reit; real-estate-valuation-l2

- **NAVPS**: estimate the market value of properties (NOI / market cap rate), add other assets, subtract liabilities, divide by shares. REITs often trade at premiums or discounts to NAV that reflect management quality and expected growth.
- **FFO** = net income + depreciation and amortization − gains (+ losses) on property sales. **AFFO** = FFO − recurring capex (and straight-line rent adjustments); closer to distributable cash. Value with **P/FFO** or **P/AFFO** relative to peers.
- **DCF/DDM**: REITs distribute most of their cash, so dividend models work; forecast growth carefully.

## Hedge fund strategy classification
@id hedge-fund-classification
@r 33
@aka hedge fund strategy classification; equity related; event driven; relative value; opportunistic; specialist; multi manager; hedge fund categories
@see hedge-funds; hedge-fund-strategies-equity; hedge-fund-strategies-event; hedge-fund-strategies-relative-value; hedge-fund-strategies-opportunistic

Hedge fund strategies are grouped by what drives their returns:

- **Equity-related**: long/short equity, dedicated short bias, equity market neutral.
- **Event-driven**: merger arbitrage, distressed securities.
- **Relative value**: fixed-income arbitrage, convertible bond arbitrage.
- **Opportunistic**: global macro, managed futures.
- **Specialist**: volatility trading, reinsurance/life settlements.
- **Multi-manager**: multi-strategy funds, funds of funds.

## Equity-related hedge fund strategies
@id hedge-fund-strategies-equity
@r 33
@aka long short equity; dedicated short bias; equity market neutral; pairs trading; stock picking hedge fund; net exposure; gross exposure
@see hedge-fund-classification

- **Long/short equity**: long undervalued, short overvalued stocks; usually net long (some market beta). Returns from stock selection; moderate leverage; good liquidity.
- **Dedicated short bias**: mostly short; negative beta; useful as a hedge but has lost money in long bull markets.
- **Equity market neutral**: balanced longs and shorts (beta ≈ 0), often quantitative or **pairs trading**; low volatility, high leverage to make returns meaningful; good diversifier but can suffer when crowded trades unwind.

## Event-driven strategies
@id hedge-fund-strategies-event
@r 33
@aka merger arbitrage; deal spread; deal risk; distressed securities; restructuring; activist; special situations; event driven strategies
@see hedge-fund-classification; private-debt

- **Merger arbitrage**: buy the target, short the acquirer (in stock deals) to earn the **deal spread**. Earns small steady gains; large losses if the deal fails, so returns are like **selling insurance (a put)** on deals, with negative skew.
- **Distressed securities**: buy debt or equity of companies in or near bankruptcy, at deep discounts; returns depend on restructuring outcomes; illiquid; long lock-ups.
- **Activist and special situations**: take stakes to push for change, or trade around spin-offs and restructurings.

## Relative value strategies
@id hedge-fund-strategies-relative-value
@r 33
@aka fixed income arbitrage; yield curve arbitrage; convertible bond arbitrage; relative value hedge funds; leverage relative value; liquidity risk arbitrage
@see hedge-fund-classification; convertible-risk-return

- **Fixed-income arbitrage**: exploit small pricing gaps between related bonds (yield curve, on-the-run vs off-the-run, swap spreads), with **high leverage**. Steady returns but exposed to liquidity crises (when spreads widen and funding dries up).
- **Convertible bond arbitrage**: buy convertibles that look cheap and short the stock (delta-hedge) to capture undervalued volatility and the bond's carry. Suffers when convertibles become illiquid or credit spreads widen.

## Opportunistic strategies: global macro and managed futures
@id hedge-fund-strategies-opportunistic
@r 33
@aka global macro; managed futures; CTA; trend following; time series momentum; crisis alpha; discretionary vs systematic
@see hedge-fund-classification

- **Global macro**: top-down discretionary bets on rates, currencies, equity indexes and commodities based on economic views. Returns are lumpy and depend on the manager; can do well in turbulent markets.
- **Managed futures (CTAs)**: mostly **systematic trend-following** in futures. Often **positively skewed** and tend to perform well in prolonged market declines ("crisis alpha"), making them strong diversifiers.

## Specialist and multi-manager strategies
@id hedge-fund-strategies-specialist
@r 33
@aka volatility trading; volatility arbitrage; reinsurance; life settlements; catastrophe bonds; multi strategy fund; fund of funds; specialist hedge fund strategies
@see hedge-fund-classification; hedge-fund-vehicles

- **Volatility trading**: trade implied vs realized volatility, relative value across volatility surfaces, or provide tail protection (long volatility).
- **Reinsurance / life settlements**: buy insurance risks (e.g. catastrophe bonds) or life insurance policies; returns uncorrelated with markets but with tail risk.
- **Multi-strategy funds**: one firm runs several strategies and reallocates capital quickly; one fee layer, but operational risk is concentrated.
- **Funds of funds**: diversify across managers; extra fees; less flexible reallocation.

## Hedge fund factor exposures and portfolio role
@id hedge-fund-factor-models
@r 33
@aka hedge fund factor model; conditional factor model; hedge fund risk exposures; hedge fund in portfolio; hedge fund diversification; adding hedge funds to 60 40; Sharpe ratio improvement
@see hedge-fund-risk-return; multifactor-models

Regress fund returns on risk factors (equity, credit spread, term spread, currency, volatility) to see where returns come from. **Conditional** factor models allow exposures to differ between normal and stressed markets, which reveals that many strategies become more correlated with equities in crises.

Adding hedge funds to a traditional stock-bond portfolio typically **lowers volatility and drawdowns** and can improve the Sharpe ratio, depending on the strategy: managed futures and macro tend to diversify best; long/short equity and event-driven add more equity-like risk.
