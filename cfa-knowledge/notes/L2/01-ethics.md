## Code and Standards at Level II: what changes
@id ethics-l2-overview
@r 40, 42
@aka level 2 ethics; code and standards level II; ethics case studies; applying the standards; ethics vignettes
@see code-and-standards; ethics-l2-application

The Code of Ethics and the seven Standards are the **same** as at Level I (see [[code-and-standards]] and each standard, e.g. [[standard-ii-a]], [[standard-iii-c]]). Level II tests them through longer case studies: you must spot which standard applies, whether conduct violates it, and what the right action is. Expect several issues in one case, firm policies to evaluate, and situations where the correct answer is "no violation".

## Applying the Code and Standards: recurring situations
@id ethics-l2-application
@r 42
@aka ethics application; common ethics violations; recommended procedures for compliance; firm policies; level II ethics cases; how to approach ethics questions
@see ethics-l2-overview; standard-iii-a; standard-iii-b; standard-vi-b; standard-ii-a

Situations that come up again and again, with the standard to check:

| Situation | Standard |
|---|---|
| Trading on a tip from a company insider, or on a takeover leak | [[standard-ii-a]] |
| Building a conclusion from public data plus non-material scraps | [[mosaic-theory]] (allowed) |
| Allocating a hot IPO to family or favoured accounts | [[standard-iii-b]], [[standard-vi-b]] |
| Using client brokerage for research that doesn't benefit the client | [[standard-iii-a]] |
| Trading ahead of a client's large order or a firm recommendation | [[standard-vi-b]] |
| Copying a colleague's or a third party's research without credit | [[standard-i-c]] |
| Taking client lists or soliciting clients before leaving a firm | [[standard-iv-a]] |
| Accepting a client bonus without employer consent | [[standard-iv-b]] |
| Not disclosing share ownership or a board seat in a report | [[standard-vi-a]] |
| A supervisor ignoring red flags about a subordinate | [[standard-iv-c]] |
| Presenting only the best accounts or simulated results as actual | [[standard-iii-d]] |
| Recommending a product without understanding it or its risks | [[standard-v-a]], [[standard-v-b]] |

Approach: identify the duty owed and to whom, check whether the action puts someone else's interest ahead of the client's, look for disclosure and consent, and remember the stricter of law and Standards applies.

## Recommended procedures: firm-level compliance
@id ethics-firm-procedures
@r 40
@aka recommended procedures for compliance; compliance program; firewall procedures; personal trading policy; restricted list; blackout periods; record keeping; written policies
@see standard-iv-c; standard-ii-a; standard-vi-b

Good firm practices that the Standards recommend (and that Level II cases often test):

- **Written compliance policies**, regularly updated, with a compliance officer.
- **Information barriers (firewalls)** between investment banking and research/trading; **restricted and watch lists**.
- **Personal trading** rules: pre-clearance, blackout periods around recommendations, reporting of holdings and trades, limits on IPO and private placement participation.
- **Fair allocation** procedures for block trades and IPOs, set in advance.
- **Disclosure** of conflicts, fee arrangements and referral fees.
- **Record retention** of research and client communications.
- **Training** and an escalation route for concerns.
