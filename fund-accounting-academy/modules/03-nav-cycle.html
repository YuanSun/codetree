The NAV Cycle

Net asset value is the product of the whole department. A senior fund accountant can explain every line of it and every movement versus yesterday.

The formula

NAV = Total assets (at fair value) − Total liabilities. Then NAV per share = NAV ÷ shares outstanding for share-based funds; for partnerships, NAV is allocated to capital accounts instead.

Gross vs net. "GAV" (gross asset value) ignores some liabilities, and many managers talk about "NAV before fees". Always confirm which definition a number uses, and whether performance fee accrual is in it.

The daily process

1 CaptureTrades from manager / OMS; corporate actions; cash movements
2 PriceSecurities, FX, curves from approved sources
3 AccrueInterest, dividends, fees, expenses, financing
4 ReconcilePositions and cash vs brokers/custodian
5 ReviewReasonableness checks on the draft NAV
6 ReleaseSign-off, distribute, archive support
  1. Capture: trades booked on trade date with correct quantity, price, fees, settle date, broker, strategy/fund allocation. Unmatched or late trades are the #1 cause of NAV errors.
  2. Price: apply the pricing policy (see Module 05). Check for stale and missing prices before they hit the NAV.
  3. Accrue: bond coupons, swap legs, borrow costs, management fee, admin fee, audit accrual.
  4. Reconcile: at least cash and positions daily where possible; the rest by month end (Module 08).
  5. Review: the checks below.
  6. Release: documented sign-off, then NAV goes to the manager (and investors/transfer agent).
Try it: NAV per share fell 3% on a day the market rose 1%. Name three things to check first.

1) Price and FX on the largest positions (stale price, wrong quote unit, wrong rate). 2) Trades and corporate actions: a duplicate trade, or a missed split or merger. 3) Accruals and liabilities: a large fee, a missing accrual reversal, or a redemption payable booked twice. Then compare P&L by position against the market.

Month-end additions

  • Final prices and FX; investor dealing: subscriptions and redemptions struck at the dealing-day NAV.
  • Management and performance fees crystallised or accrued per terms; allocation of P&L to investors.
  • Full position / cash / third-party reconciliations; clearing old breaks.
  • Expense true-ups (audit, legal, organizational costs amortization).
  • Investor statements, financial reporting packages, trial balance review.

Reasonableness checks on a draft NAV

CheckWhat it catches
NAV change vs expected return (manager estimate or benchmark)Missing trade, bad price, wrong FX
Top positions: price move vs marketStale or mistyped price; wrong currency
P&L by position, largest contributors and detractorsOutliers that explain the move
Cash by currency vs bank / broker, negative balancesUnbooked settlement, FX trade missing
Accruals vs prior day, interest vs rate × balanceMissing or doubled accrual
Capital activity: shares, subs, reds vs transfer agentDealing errors
Flat-price check: prices unchanged for N daysStale pricing
Exposure vs mandate (concentration, leverage)Booking errors; also compliance flags to escalate

When the NAV is wrong

An error discovered after release is a NAV error. Typical steps: quantify the impact by day, check against the fund's materiality threshold (often 0.5% of NAV for equity funds, lower for money-market style funds; it is set by policy, not by you), notify the manager, determine who is compensated (fund vs investors who dealt at the wrong price), correct, and document root cause and prevention.

Never silently fix. Corrections must be visible and approved. A hidden adjustment, even a small one, is a control failure.

Estimate vs final NAV

Hedge funds often publish an estimate a few days after month end and a final weeks later after statements arrive. Understand the tolerance for differences, and which investors transacted at which.
