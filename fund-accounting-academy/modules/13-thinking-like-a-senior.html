Thinking Like a Senior

Seniority is less about knowing more instruments and more about judgement: what to check, what to question, when to escalate, and how to leave the process better than you found it.

From preparer to reviewer

A preparer asks "did I enter it correctly?" A reviewer asks "would I notice if this were wrong?" Reviewing is a skill with a method:

  1. Start from expectation: before opening the numbers, estimate the answer (NAV change from benchmark moves, fee from NAV × rate).
  2. Look at movement, not just balances: day-on-day and month-on-month; what changed and why.
  3. Prioritise by risk: size × complexity × novelty. New instruments, new counterparties, manual overrides, and Level 3 deserve more time.
  4. Test the controls, not just the output: was the sign-off done, were the exceptions cleared?
  5. Leave evidence: a note saying what you checked is the product of the review.

Materiality and judgement

  • Materiality has quantitative (percent of NAV or of an investor's balance) and qualitative aspects (a small error that hides a breach, a related-party issue, or a trend).
  • Thresholds come from policy and the manager/board; you apply and escalate, you don't invent them.
  • When in doubt, escalate early with facts: what, size, cause, proposed fix, deadline.

Escalation template

What:      NAV for Fund X on 12-Mar is overstated by $182k (0.31% of NAV)
Cause:     Corporate action (2-for-1 split in ABC) not processed on ex-date
Impact:    Dealt on 12-Mar: 2 subscriptions ($1.1m), 1 redemption ($0.4m)
Proposed:  Back-date CA, re-strike NAV, compensate per policy; fund materiality threshold 0.5%
Need from you: approval to proceed; manager confirmation of position
Deadline:  Today 15:00 (NAV distribution cut-off)

Communication with managers, investors and auditors

  • Be specific and neutral: "Position differs by 5,000 shares vs PB; likely unbooked trade of 3 Mar; please confirm" beats "your trade is missing".
  • Own the timeline: "I will update by 14:00."
  • Separate fact from view: valuation views are the manager's, evidence is yours.
  • Write for the auditor: any adjustment should be explainable without you in the room.

Ownership of the close

  • Run the close from a calendar with named owners and cut-offs, working backwards from the NAV release.
  • Track open items on a visible list with age and owner.
  • Hold a short retrospective on every miss: cause, fix, prevention, owner.

Improving the process

  • Automate repetitive checks (price tolerance reports, rec auto-match, capital tie-out).
  • Standardise templates and checklists across funds.
  • Document tribal knowledge: one-page procedures for the 20 things only one person knows.
  • Measure: NAV on time %, errors per 100 NAVs, aged breaks, rework hours.

Developing your career

LevelFocus
AssociateAccurate processing, learning systems, clearing breaks.
SeniorReviewing, complex instruments, investor-level calcs, training others.
ManagerPeople, client relationship, process design, risk.

Credentials that help: CPA/ACCA, CFA (levels I–II give a valuable investment foundation), and CAIA for alternatives. Pair them with deep system skills (Geneva, plus SQL/Excel/Python for analysis).

A 90-day plan Month 1: master your funds' daily checklists and reconcile everything you touch. Month 2: lead one month-end including fees and capital allocation. Month 3: find one recurring break, fix its root cause, and document the new process.
