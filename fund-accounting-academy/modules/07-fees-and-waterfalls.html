Fees, Expenses & Waterfalls

Fees are contractual math applied to NAV. Mistakes here create investor complaints, so read the offering document, not just the system configuration.

Management fee

Fee = NAV × annual rate × days ÷ basis (365 or 360; or rate ÷ 12 per month). Things the documents specify:

  • Which NAV: opening, closing, average, before or after accruing performance fee, before or after redemptions.
  • Paid monthly or quarterly, in advance or arrears.
  • Treatment of side pockets (often a lower rate).
  • Fee on committed capital vs invested capital (private funds).

Performance (incentive) fee

Charged on profit above specified hurdles. The key terms:

TermMeaning
Fee rateTypically 10–30% of profits above thresholds (20% is classic).
High-water mark (HWM)No fee until the investor's NAV exceeds its highest prior end-of-period value (after fees). Losses must be recovered first.
Hurdle rateMinimum return before fee. Soft hurdle: once cleared, fee on all profit above HWM. Hard hurdle: fee only on the excess over the hurdle.
CrystallizationWhen accrued fee becomes payable (annually, quarterly, or on redemption).
Loss carry-forwardAlternative to HWM at investor level for shared accounts.
ClawbackReturn of previously paid incentive if later losses exceed (typical in PE).

Worked example (soft vs hard hurdle)

Opening NAV 100, HWM 100, closing before fee 110, hurdle 5%, rate 20%.

  • No hurdle: chargeable = 10 → fee 2.00.
  • Soft 5% hurdle: hurdle level 105 is cleared; chargeable = 110 − 100 = 10 → fee 2.00.
  • Hard 5% hurdle: chargeable = 110 − 105 = 5 → fee 1.00.
  • HWM 120: 110 < 120 → fee 0; the new HWM stays 120.

Redemptions mid-period. A redeemer crystallizes the accrued fee on the redeemed part. Many errors come from forgetting that the fee on redeemed capital is paid out on that date.

Fund expenses

  • Operating: admin, audit, legal, directors, custody, registered office, regulatory fees.
  • Trading-related: commissions, financing, borrow, interest.
  • Organizational costs: expensed (US GAAP) or amortised (some jurisdictions/IFRS variations); offering costs are typically deferred and amortised for a defined period.
  • Expense caps / waivers: manager reimburses above a cap; recorded as a receivable or expense reduction.

Accrue to a budget daily and true-up when invoices arrive; large true-ups are a signal that the accrual basis was wrong.

Private-fund distribution waterfalls

Proceeds are applied in tiers. A common European (whole-fund) waterfall:

  1. Return of capital: LPs get contributed capital back first.
  2. Preferred return: e.g. 8% compounded annually to LPs.
  3. GP catch-up: GP receives 100% (or e.g. 50%) until it has carried interest = 20% of profit so far.
  4. Residual split: 80/20 (LP/GP).

An American (deal-by-deal) waterfall applies the tiers to each investment as it's realised, which pays the GP sooner and usually requires a clawback.

Example: LP contributes 100, three years, 8% pref, 20% carry, 100% catch-up, distributions total 200. Tier 1: 100. Tier 2 pref = 100×(1.08³−1) = 25.97. Tier 3 catch-up = 0.2×25.97 ÷ (1−0.2) = 6.49 to GP. Tier 4: remaining 67.54 split 80/20. GP total = 6.49 + 13.51 = 20.00 = 20% of the 100 profit. The waterfall calculator does this for you.
