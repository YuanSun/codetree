Instruments & How to Book Them

For each instrument ask four questions: What is the position? What is the cash? What accrues daily? What does the broker statement show?

Equities (long and short)

  • Long: buy at cost, carry at fair value, dividends from ex-date.
  • Short: borrow and sell. The position is a liability (securities sold short) at fair value. Proceeds are held by the PB. You owe dividends and pay a borrow fee on the market value. Gains come from falling prices.
  • Commission and fees affect cost (buys) or proceeds (sells).

Bonds and accrued interest

Bonds are quoted clean (without accrued interest). You pay dirty = clean + accrued. The accrued amount you pay a seller is not an expense; it's recorded as interest receivable purchased and netted against the first coupon.

Accrued interest = Par × coupon rate × (days accrued ÷ day-count basis).

Also: premium/discount amortization (straight-line or effective yield per policy), OID, inflation-linked principal adjustments, and paydowns on amortizing securities (reduce face and cost proportionally).

FX spot and forwards

  • Spot: settles in T+2 for most pairs. Book on trade date.
  • Forward: value = (agreed forward rate − current forward rate for the remaining term) × notional, discounted. The unrealized P&L appears as an asset or liability. At maturity it becomes realized when the currencies exchange.
  • Hedged share classes: the forward's P&L is allocated only to the hedged class.

Futures

Futures carry no purchase price; you post initial margin (an asset held with the broker, not a cost) and receive/pay variation margin daily. Daily variation margin settles P&L in cash, so the future's fair value after settlement is effectively zero and the gain flows through realized/unrealized P&L. Remember contract multiplier (e.g. S&P e-mini = $50 × index).

Options

  • Buying: premium paid is cost; mark-to-market via market price or model. Expire worthless → realized loss of premium.
  • Writing: premium received creates a liability at fair value; closing or expiry realizes the difference.
  • Exercise/assignment: option cost/proceeds rolls into the cost of the underlying acquired or the proceeds of the underlying sold.
  • Check: multiplier (100 for equity options), expiry dates (and after-hours exercise), early assignment risk on short in-the-money calls around ex-dividend.

Common pitfalls: reset dates, notional changes after partial unwinds, mismatch between the dealer's valuation and the fund's model.

Repos, loans, and others

Repo is a collateralized loan: the cash lent/borrowed accrues interest, the collateral isn't derecognised. Bank loans have floating rates with resets, PIK interest and agency fee notices. Treat each as "cash + schedule + accrual" and tie out to the agent's notices.

Habit Maintain a corporate-action calendar for each fund's holdings. Most nasty NAV errors are unprocessed corporate actions discovered when a position break appears.
