Pricing & Fair Value

Price is the largest single driver of NAV. A senior accountant doesn't just load prices, they can defend them.

Where prices come from

  • Vendors (Bloomberg, ICE, Refinitiv, Markit, etc.): exchange closes and evaluated prices for OTC instruments.
  • Brokers / dealers: quotes for illiquid bonds, loans, derivatives.
  • Manager marks: for hard-to-value assets, subject to the fund's valuation policy and administrator challenge.
  • Models: DCF, comparables, option models. Inputs must be observable where possible.

The valuation policy defines primary and backup sources, cut-off time (e.g. 4 p.m. NY), which side of the market to use (bid for longs, ask for shorts, or mid), and who can override.

The fair value hierarchy (ASC 820 / IFRS 13)

LevelInputsExamples
Level 1Quoted prices in active markets for identical assetsListed equities, open-ended mutual funds with daily NAV, exchange-traded futures
Level 2Observable inputs other than Level 1 quotesCorporate bonds priced by evaluated vendor, OTC FX forwards, swaps with observable curves
Level 3Unobservable inputs; significant judgementPrivate equity stakes, distressed loans, illiquid structured credit

The level is determined by the lowest significant input. Level 3 positions need a rollforward and disclosure of unobservable inputs in the financial statements.

NAV-as-practical-expedient. Investments in other funds can be valued at the investee's reported NAV per share and disclosed separately from the hierarchy when the practical expedient is used (ASC 820-10-35-59).

Price challenge process

  1. Exception report: no price, stale price (unchanged for N days), large move vs prior day, move vs peers.
  2. Compare against a second source. Investigate corporate actions, currency, quote basis (clean/dirty, % of par vs absolute).
  3. Escalate to the manager with evidence, not opinion: "Vendor A 98.5, Vendor B 98.4, your mark 101.0 — please support."
  4. Where the manager insists, document the override, approver, rationale, and take it to the pricing/valuation committee if the policy requires.

Frequent pricing traps

  • Quote units: UK equities in pence (GBp) vs pounds; bonds quoted as % of par; futures with tick values.
  • Stale prices on holidays; thinly traded names; delisted or suspended securities (price may be zero or last).
  • FX timing: FX rate cut (e.g. WM/Reuters 4pm London) must match policy.
  • Bid/ask vs last: consistent application matters more than which is "right".
  • Same security, two identifiers (CUSIP/ISIN/SEDOL/FIGI) → duplicate or missing prices.

Pricing committee & independence

The administrator's value is independent verification. If the manager both provides and approves marks for Level 3 assets, make sure the administrator receives evidence (valuation reports, third-party appraisals) and records the review. For difficult cases, auditors will ask for this trail.
