Reconciliation & Controls

Reconciliation is where your books meet reality. If you can't prove the book to an independent source, you don't know your NAV — you're guessing.

The anatomy of a break

  1. Identify: security, quantity or amount difference, currency, as-of date.
  2. Classify: timing (settlement lag), data (wrong price/FX/identifier), missing event (corporate action or trade), or genuine error on either side.
  3. Resolve: book the entry, or ask the counterparty to correct. Keep evidence.
  4. Age and escalate: breaks older than policy thresholds (e.g. 5 / 30 / 60 days) go to a manager and appear in the control report.
Rule of thumb Fix the cause, not the symptom. If the same break type recurs, change the process (an automated feed, a checklist, an instruction to the broker).

Controls framework

  • Segregation of duties: whoever inputs doesn't approve. Four-eyes review on NAV, trades above threshold, manual journal entries, standing data changes.
  • Preventive vs detective: system validation (balanced entries, mandatory fields) vs reconciliations and exception reports.
  • Evidence: every control leaves a dated sign-off and a retained report.
  • Access: role-based permissions; periodic user access reviews; offboarding is immediate.
  • Change management: system configuration changes (fee rules, allocation rules, report logic) tested and approved before production.
  • Business continuity: tested recovery; documented manual fall-back for the NAV.

SOC 1 / ISAE 3402

Administrators often undergo a SOC 1 Type II audit (ISAE 3402 abroad): an auditor tests the design and operating effectiveness of control objectives over a period. Clients' auditors rely on it. As a practitioner you'll be asked for evidence of control operation: keep sign-offs complete and timely, because a missed sign-off is a deviation even when the underlying work was right.

Common failure patterns

  • "Plugging" a break with a miscellaneous account, which hides issues.
  • Rec performed on a different date from the NAV (trade-date books vs settle-date broker positions).
  • Relying on one source for both price and reconciliation.
  • Workarounds that live in spreadsheets without review.
  • Staff turnover: process knowledge not documented.
