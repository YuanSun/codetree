Geneva: Daily & Month-End Workflow

A repeatable routine is what makes a senior fund accountant fast and safe. This is a template you adapt to your firm's procedures.

Month-end additions

  1. Final pricing and FX at the month-end cut; resolve all challenges.
  2. Investor activity: record subscriptions and redemptions; confirm with the transfer agent / bank.
  3. Fees: run management and performance fee calculations; reconcile to your independent calc (Module 07).
  4. Allocation: run the P&L allocation to investors; confirm capital accounts sum to NAV.
  5. True-ups: expenses, amortisation, accrual reversals.
  6. Review package: trial balance, appraisal, P&L, capital rollforward, rec status, aged breaks.
  7. Close the period: lock it once approved so no late changes sneak in.
  8. Deliver NAV, statements, support schedules.

Corrections after the fact

With a bitemporal system you can enter a back-dated transaction without overwriting history. The safe routine:

  1. Find the root cause and confirm in the audit trail how the data got there.
  2. Check whether the period is closed; reopen only with approval and document it.
  3. Enter the correction with the right effective date; Geneva records today as the knowledge date.
  4. Re-run affected reports at the old and new knowledge dates to quantify impact by day; take it to the NAV-error process (Module 03).
  5. Re-run allocations and fees for any period affected.
Order matters. Fix upstream data first (trades, prices, corporate actions), then recalc accruals, fees and allocations. Fixing allocations before the portfolio-level error is resolved just moves the problem around.

Speed habits for seniors

  • Keep saved report templates with parameters set (period, knowledge date, basis, currency).
  • Build a standing exception list: items that always need attention (illiquid prices, FX forwards, swap resets).
  • Keep a day-by-day change log for each fund: "what was different this month".
  • Write the "why" in the transaction comment/field where available; your future self and auditor will thank you.

Find it in your Geneva

  1. Where do trade files land, and which job/process loads them? What does a rejected record look like?
  2. Where are prices loaded; what are the primary/backup sources; what is the exception report called?
  3. How is the period locked, who can reopen it, and how is that logged?
  4. Write your own one-page "daily checklist" with real screen and report names.
